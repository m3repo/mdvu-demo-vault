# The Story of Schlauraffen Land

> [!info] Grimm's Household Tales, No. 158
> German title: *Das Märchen vom Schlauraffenland* · translated by Margaret Hunt (1884)

> [!abstract] In short
> A narrator tells of his visit to Schlauraffen Land, where absurd sights follow one after another, and openly declares that he is telling lies. The tale ends with a cock crowing as a frame.

## Entry into the land

In the time of Schlauraffen I went there, and saw Rome and the Lateran hanging by a small silken thread, and a man without feet who outran a swift horse, and a keen sharp sword that cut through a bridge.

## Impossible sights

There I saw a young ass with a silver nose which pursued two fleet hares, and a lime-tree that was very large, on which hot cakes were growing.

## Lean goat and the plough

There I saw a lean old goat which carried about a hundred cart-loads of fat on his body, and sixty loads of salt. Have I not told enough lies? There I saw a plough ploughing without horse or cow, and

## The child and the millstones

a child of one year threw four millstones from Ratisbon to Treves, and from Treves to Strasburg, and a hawk swam over the Rhine, which he had a perfect right to do. There I heard some fishes begin to make such a disturbance with each other, that it resounded as far as heaven, and sweet honey flowed like water from a deep valley at the top of a high mountain, and these were strange things. There were two crows which were mowing a meadow, and I saw two gnats building a bridge, and two doves tore a wolf to pieces; two children brought forth two kids, and two frogs threshed corn together. There I saw two mice consecrating a bishop, and two cats scratching out a bear’s tongue.

## Snail and lions

Then a snail came running up and killed two furious lions. There stood a barber and shaved a woman’s beard off; and two sucking-children bade their mother hold her tongue. There I saw two greyhounds which brought a mill out of the water; and a sorry old horse was beside it, and said it was right. And four horses were standing in the yard threshing corn with all their might, and two goats were heating the stove, and a red cow shot the bread into the oven.

## Closing crow

Then a cock crowed, Cock-a-doodle-doo! The story is all told,—Cock-a-doodle-doo!
