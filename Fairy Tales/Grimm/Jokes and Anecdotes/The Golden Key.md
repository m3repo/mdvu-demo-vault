# The Golden Key

> [!info] Grimm's Household Tales, No. 200
> German title: *Der goldene Schlüssel* · translated by Margaret Hunt (1884)

> [!abstract] In short
> A poor boy gathering firewood in the snow finds a tiny golden key and then an iron chest with a very small keyhole. He turns the key, and the tale ends before the chest is opened.

## The Winter Errand

In the winter time, when deep snow lay on the ground, a poor boy was forced to go out on a sledge to fetch wood. When he had gathered it together, and packed it, he wished, as he was so frozen with cold, not to go home at once, but to light a fire and warm himself a little.

## The Tiny Key

So he scraped away the snow, and as he was thus clearing the ground, he found a tiny, gold key.

## The Iron Chest

Hereupon he thought that where the key was, the lock must be also, and dug in the ground and found an iron chest. “If the key does but fit it!” thought he; “no doubt there are precious things in that little box.”

## The Hidden Keyhole

He searched, but no keyhole was there. At last he discovered one, but so small that it was hardly visible. He tried it, and the key fitted it exactly. Then he turned it once round, and now we must wait until he has quite unlocked it and opened the lid, and then we shall learn what wonderful things were lying in that box.
