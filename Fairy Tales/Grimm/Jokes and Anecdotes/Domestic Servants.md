# Domestic Servants

> [!info] Grimm's Household Tales, No. 140
> German title: *Das Hausgesinde* · translated by Margaret Hunt (1884)

> [!abstract] In short
> A chant of call and response about travelling to Walpe, naming a man, a child, a cradle and a drudge. It has no narrative actors and works as a cumulative jest.

## Opening exchange: Walpe

“Whither goest thou?” “To Walpe.” “I to Walpe, thou to Walpe, so, so, together we’ll go.”

## The man Cham and the child Wild

“Hast thou a man? What is his name?” “Cham.” “My man Cham, thy man Cham; I to Walpe, thou to Walpe; so, so, together we’ll go.” “Hast thou a child; how is he styled?” “Wild.” “My child Wild, thy child Wild; my man Cham, thy man Cham; I to Walpe, thou to Walpe, so, so, together we’ll go.”

## The cradle Hippodadle

“Hast thou a cradle? How callest thou thy cradle?” “Hippodadle.” “My cradle Hippodadle, my child Wild, thy child Wild, my man Cham, thy man Cham; I to Walpe, thou to Walpe, so, so, together we’ll go.”

## The drudge

“Hast thou also a drudge? what name has thy drudge?” “From-thy-work-do-not-budge.”

## Refrain and ending

“My drudge, From-thy-work-do-not-budge: my child Wild, thy child Wild; my man Cham, thy man Cham; I to Walpe, thou to Walpe; so, so, together we’ll go.”
